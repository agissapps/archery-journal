Where does your hand end?

How the string hand ends after the release and what it tells you about your shot.

Clean

The hand slides back along the neck, no more than about 3 cm (along the face, in barebow). It looks like the continuation of expansion and not like opening the hand on purpose [1]. It comes from back tension, not from the fingers [1].

Plucking

The hand comes off the face during the shot. That pulls the back of the arrow outward and, for a right-handed archer, arrows go left [2]. The fix the source gives: keep the string hand tight against the face [2].

Collapse

If the drawing arm isn't drawn far enough or power is lost, the elbow creeps forward and the string moves forward. The balance of expansion is lost and the arrow goes left or right [1]. Stay loose in the upper body and let the power come from the ground [1].

What it is

After the release, the string hand travels somewhere. That path is the follow-through and it tells a lot about how the release was made [1].

Here we explain the endings that the sources we could read describe. In our videos we talk about five; below we say which ones we couldn't find backed up.

Other release details

Things the sources say about the release that help you read your ending:

How a tension trainer helps
A back-tension trainer with an elastic (non-rigid) element helps the follow-through run to a natural stop. Rigid ones are discouraged because they force freezing or tension [1].
The finger hook
The hook should be set at the start and not readjusted at anchor, because readjusting adds hand and forearm tension and ruins the release [1].
Forcing the release
Forcing the release too much sends the arrow right, and it can show up as a bare shaft landing right in the test [2]. Keep the release relaxed and natural, without forcing the follow-through [2].
Finger pressure
More pressure above the arrow sends it high and more pressure below sends it low [2].
Watch the string on your chest
With correct back tension, the string doesn't move on the chest. If you see it move, something changed [1].

What the sources don't cover

In our videos we also talk about the “dead release” (the hand stays at the chin because you open your fingers yourself) and the “rising elbow” (groups moving up and down). None of the sources we could read describes them, so we don't explain them here or give a fix. If you see them in your shooting, a coach can check them with you.

In the app: Posture records your shot and replays it in slow motion so you can see where your hand ends.

Heads up: The sources give mostly text, not figures: the only number is that the hand moves back no more than about 3 cm [1]. The other endings from our videos, with no backing, are not explained here.
