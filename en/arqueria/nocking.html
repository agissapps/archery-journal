Nocking point

The spot on the string where the arrow sits, how it's measured, tied and fine-tuned.

Measure

The bow square slides down the string until its arm touches the arrow rest, at 90°. That is the zero line; the nocking point is marked a little above it [1].

Bare shaft lands high

If the bare shaft lands above the group, the nocking point is too low: raise it [2].

Bare shaft lands low

If the bare shaft lands below the group, the nocking point is too high: lower it [2].

What it is

It is the mark on the string where the arrow is set. It makes the arrow go to the same place every time and leave the same way on every shot [3][4].

It can be tied with serving thread or be a brass point clamped onto the string; both are valid and it is a matter of taste [3]. Ready-made plastic or metal ones also exist [4].

At least one is used. With two (one above and one below the nock) the arrow is well positioned; leave enough room for the arrow to move so they don't pinch it [3][4].

Where it goes

A bow square is clipped on the string and slid until its arm touches the arrow rest, at 90°. That is the zero line. The nocking point goes a little above it [1].

The starting height changes by source. Archery 360: Olympic recurve and traditional, ⅜″ high; barebow, ½″ (or ⅛″ with a drop-away rest). World Archery: ³⁄₁₆″ (about 5 mm) above the square's line. 60X Custom Strings: ⅛″ to ³⁄₁₆″, starting at ⅛″ [3][4][1].

That's why it's only a starting point: you fine-tune later with a bare shaft.

How it's tied

Archery 360 gives this method for tying it with thread [3]:

  1. Get your materialServing thread (about 60 cm, two feet), a lighter, super glue and the bow square [3].
  2. Mark the zero lineClip the bow square on the string and slide it until its arm touches the arrow rest. Tie a single overhand knot to mark that spot [3].
  3. Tie the knotsTie alternating double overhand knots, one on each side of the string, until you have six per side. Finish with two single overhand knots [3].
  4. FinishCut the tails, melt them with the lighter and add a drop of glue [3].
  5. Set the bottom oneWith a single overhand knot, about 1/16″ below the nock, so it doesn't pinch [3].

Heads up: The sources don't agree on the starting height, and none gives the fine-tuning increments beyond “small steps”. Do it with an archery technician the first time.
