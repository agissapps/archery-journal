Your 6 numbers

The six numbers of your setup worth writing down and how each is obtained.

Draw length

The ATA standard measures draw length to the grip's pivot point and adds 1¾ inches; manufacturers use that figure [1][2]. The best way to measure it, according to Archery 360, is with a technician at a shop [3].

Arrow length

It is measured from the nock to a little past the arrow rest, and it isn't cut too short. The margin changes by guide: Legend Archery says most archers leave between half an inch and one inch beyond the arrow rest [4].

String

On a recurve the string is usually 3 to 4 inches shorter than the bow [5].

Tiller

It is the difference in distance between the string and the top and bottom limbs, measured from the base of the limbs at 90° [1].

What they are for

They are six numbers that help you buy gear, tune it and compare your results over time. Write them down and check they're still the same when you change something.

The six, one by one

1 · Draw length
It is your full-draw distance measured with the ATA standard: to the grip's pivot point plus 1¾″ [1][2]. Have a technician measure it [3].
2 · Arrow length
It is measured from the nock to a little past the arrow rest; most archers leave half an inch to one inch [4]. Our suggestion: don't cut it shorter than where it touches the clicker or the arrow rest; if it would end up shorter, buy other arrows.
3 · Real draw weight
The weight marked on the limbs is measured at the 28″ standard ATA draw length [6]. If your draw is different, the weight at your fingers changes; the rule of thumb often quoted is about 2 pounds per inch, which we couldn't verify in a specific source.
4 · Spine
It comes from the manufacturer's chart or selector, using real draw weight, length and point [7]. See the “Spine” page.
5 · String
On a recurve it is usually 3 to 4 inches shorter than the bow [5]. Check your bow's manual.
6 · Tiller
It is the distance from the string to the top limb and the bottom limb, measured from the base of the limbs at 90°. Hoyt recommends starting with the tiller equal top and bottom and adjusting it with the limb-weight bolts [1].

In the app: The gear assistant guides you through draw weight, draw length, brace height and tiller, and you can save your arrows with their spine.

Heads up: The readable sources don't say how to calculate draw weight at another draw length or give typical tiller values for recurve, and Hoyt's definition speaks about bows in general. Some recurve archers leave the top limb slightly more open than the bottom one, but no source we can cite backs it. Confirm everything with your bow's manual or your coach.
